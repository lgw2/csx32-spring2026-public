\begin{enumerate}


\item In class, we gave a hand-wavy argument for why the Euclidian Algorithm, $gcd(a,b)$, has logarithmic runtime in terms of the values of $a$ or $b$.
In this problem, you will give a worst-case analysis of this fact.

\begin{enumerate}
	\item What goes in the blank space to complete the recursive definition of the $k$-th Fibonacci number, $F_k$? The base cases are given for you.
\[
F_k =
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } k = 0 \\
2 & \text{if } k = 1 \\
\underline{\hspace{1in}} & \text{if } k > 1
\end{cases}
\]


\item Use induction to show that the smallest \emph{size} input to $gcd(a,b)$
that takes $k$ recursive calls to compute is $F_k, F_{k-1}$. If it helps,
you can assume that $a > b$ and $b > 0$. You can use the following to structure your proof
as we did earlier in this course.

\begin{itemize}
    \item What is the universal declaration? (Let ... be an arbitrary...)
    \item What is the inductive hypothesis? (Assume ... for all $k' < k$)
    \item What is the proof of the base case(s)? Make sure that it is clear
    \emph{what you are trying to prove} and that you have proved it.
    \item What is the proof of the inductive case? Again, make sure it is clear
    \emph{what you are trying to prove} and that you have proved it (using the inductive hypothesis, of course!).
    
\end{itemize}

\item You have now shown that you 
any input to $gcd(a,b)$ where $N=a+b$ is less than $F_{k+1}$ 
will take $k$ recursive calls to compute. 
Using the fact that $F_{k+1} \approx 1.618^{k+1}$, derive a tight asymptotic
bound on the runtime of $gcd(a,b)$ in terms of $N$.

\item Finally, convert your tight asymptotic bound on the runtime of $gcd(a,b)$
in terms of $N$ into a tight asymptotic bound in terms of the number of bits
needed to represent $a$ and $b$. (Hint: how many bits are needed to represent a
number $N$?)
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
